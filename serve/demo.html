Why Your Feedback Process Is Broken

Draft — open for review

The Feedback Graveyard

Every team has one. It is the Google Doc with forty-seven unresolved comments, the pull request where three reviewers gave contradictory advice, the shared document where someone wrote "needs work" six months ago and nobody remembers what they meant. Feedback goes in. Nothing comes out.

The problem is not that people fail to give feedback. Most teams are drowning in it. The problem is that collecting feedback and acting on feedback are treated as two separate activities, performed by different people, at different times, with no connecting tissue between them.

The Missing Step

Between "reviewer leaves a comment" and "author revises the document" there is a gap. Someone has to read every comment, understand the intent, reconcile conflicting suggestions, and produce a coherent revision. This synthesis step is invisible, thankless, and almost entirely manual.

For a document with ten comments, this takes an hour. For one with fifty, it takes a day. For a living document that accumulates feedback continuously, it never ends. The author becomes a full-time comment processor instead of a writer. The feedback loop is not closed — it is a feedback line, and it only goes in one direction.

"We have more tools for leaving feedback than ever before, and less process for acting on it. The bottleneck moved, but nobody noticed."

What Closing the Loop Looks Like

Imagine a different workflow. A reviewer highlights a sentence and writes: "This claim needs a citation." Within seconds, an AI agent reads the comment, understands the context, drafts a revision with a proper reference, and posts its response in the same thread. The reviewer sees the change and either approves it or pushes back with a follow-up comment.

No context switching. No synthesis step. No waiting for the author to get around to it. The feedback loop closes in real time, while the reviewer's intent is still fresh. The author still has final say — the agent proposes, the human disposes — but the manual drudgery of processing comments one by one is gone.

This is not a hypothetical. It is what Remarq does. One script tag makes any web page commentable. An AI agent watches for new comments and responds automatically. The feedback-to-revision cycle that used to take days now takes seconds.
